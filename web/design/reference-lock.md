## Original section hierarchy and availability

The original direction pairs a rescue scene with a readable warm-paper field guide. The open route-line motif links a hazard, a deliberate intervention and residents reaching safety. It is original simple linework, never a rescue-service emblem or medical cross. Keep the site's visual energy in the real little world; the surrounding information is calm and direct.

1. **Navigation:** RESQ wordmark; How to play; Worlds; Accessibility; Press. Support and Privacy remain in the footer and directly accessible. On desktop these are visible links. The mobile disclosure contains the same destinations with keyboard/focus support.
2. **Hero:** “Small worlds. Big saves.” One truthful sentence explaining rescue strategy. A real qualified close-up must visibly connect danger → intervention → a route to safety. One primary action depends on actual evidence; a secondary working link leads to `/how-to-play`.
3. **Read. Plan. Act. Rescue.** Four sequential beats, each with a specific decision and consequence. Use wide media/text rows rather than four identical feature cards. Explain that residents and occupied objects cannot be grabbed to bypass strategy.
4. **One place, two approaches.** Show two qualified strategies only when their exact replay/capture evidence exists. Annotated arrows are labeled editorial overlays. Until then this is a future content requirement, not a validated claim.
5. **Five worlds. Twenty-five missions.** Five place-led chapters with explicit planned/implemented/qualified/released state, hazard summary and mission links. Current internal drafts label all five planned and unqualified. A public first-chapter edition segregates future plans or omits them from navigation; the finished full-product site includes all five qualified worlds.
6. **Find your way to play.** A plain-language path to `/accessibility` covering hands, one-hand completion, separate head-dwell Command, controllers, seated setup and fallback. Every public claim must match the exact tested release.
7. **A closer look:** ten real captioned screenshots, two per world, with source-build/world/mode evidence. A small sequential gallery can be used; all images remain reachable without drag gestures.
8. **Footer:** working available next step, Support, Accessibility, Privacy, Press and verified owner/credits. Intended `playresq.com` is not a verified owned host.

For the present unqualified state, “Read the planned game” can link to a real local content page during later development. Do not render “Play now”, store badges, “Watch gameplay”, downloads, contact or archive actions before their actual destination/media exists. When qualified approved footage exists, the primary action can become “Watch gameplay” with a working local player; publication and store availability are separate gates. Missing public support identity/contact is a content prerequisite, never permission to invent an address. There are no accounts, waitlists, email forms, fake success states, checkout, CMS, analytics or tracking services in this scope.

## Annotated original layouts

**Desktop, 1440px viewport:** 1280px maximum content within 40px minimum gutters. A 72px header occupies its own solid paper row. The media-led hero uses a full-width 16:9 scene with a constrained height of `min(56.25vw, 720px)` and no forced full-screen lock. The image's meaningful rescue action remains visible; letterbox rather than crop out the cause or rescued residents. Directly below, a solid paper rail places title/summary in columns 1–8 and real actions in columns 10–12, 32px vertical padding. This original rail deliberately resolves the variable contrast of the A24 overlay.

The story starts 96px after the hero rail. Narrative sections use the 12-column grid with 24px gaps, seven columns of scene and five columns of explanation, 60–68ch maximum prose. A caption sits directly under each image. A small numbered label names each decision; decorative route lines never carry information alone. Worlds switch to a chapter rhythm: place heading, one image/story pair, five-mission ordered list, then a 96px boundary before the next world. Avoid endlessly repeating rows of three cards. Support/Privacy use a 240px contents rail and a readable 68ch article column with generous empty space to the right.

**Tablet, 768px:** 24px gutters; retain a split only while the prose has at least 32ch usable measure. Otherwise stack in semantic order. Navigation may wrap or use the disclosure when its actual content does not fit. No fixed-height text containers.

**Mobile, 320/360px:** 16px gutters. Header is 64px minimum with a wordmark and labeled Menu disclosure. Hero scene is followed by H1, description, status and vertically stacked actions. At 320px the inner width is 288px; headings wrap naturally and controls are 48px minimum high. Story order is heading → media → caption → explanation → mission list. World imagery uses `contain` if a crop loses the rescue relationship. Support opens with an inline contents list, followed by every article; no modal, hidden side panel or horizontal tabs are needed to read essential help. Footer links wrap into a plain vertical list.

**200% text and long content:** use rem-based type, intrinsic row heights, wrapping labels and breakable long URLs. Collapse columns based on available space. No clip, ellipsis or horizontal scroll on essential prose or actions. Native anchor targets receive scroll margin and visible keyboard focus. These are design requirements; browser verification belongs to D04/D05 and FULL-WEB, not F08.

## Motion, media and interaction requirements

No hero autoplay or background audio. A qualified still is the default; request the real clip only after user Play, with native pause/seek/volume, captions and a readable equivalent. `preload="none"`; never download all five world clips on first view. If unavailable/slow, retain the poster, explanatory fallback and the rest of the page; no spinner traps or fake media buttons. If no qualified poster exists, the future site must show an explicit text-only unavailable state rather than concept/reference art posing as gameplay.

Normal interface transitions use 160ms `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)` opacity/color changes. An optional original decorative route line can reveal once in 240ms. No parallax, scroll-linked transformation, continuous floating or automatic carousel. Reduced motion removes line reveal, animated entrances and smooth scrolling, and keeps posters as default. User-requested video remains user-controlled. Every video requires pause, captions/transcript where appropriate, and focus behavior. Galleries use labeled previous/next buttons plus direct image access; dialog close restores focus and Escape works. Search is omitted unless real local indexing, results and no-results behavior are implemented/tested; the contents links are sufficient for support.

Future media IDs should map to exact qualified source manifests: `hero-loop`, `hero-poster`, `world-{slug}-clip`, `world-{slug}-poster`, `world-{slug}-still-01/02`, `trailer-160`, `trailer-captions`, `press-archive`, `factsheet`, `credits`, `support-guide`. Clips are 8–12 seconds per world; there are five clips/posters and ten cleared stills. Each requires build/world/mode, capture identity, rights/consent, hashes and responsive derivatives. The hero shows a real cause/effect rescue sequence; montage must not combine planned worlds with qualified footage. Wordmark/Q exports come from approved original brand assets. Research captures and font files are not substitutes for any game media slot.
